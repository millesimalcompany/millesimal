The Millesimal Standard

Seven criteria. All seven, or it was not a qualified meeting.

A public definition of a qualified outbound meeting, written by a party with no stake in whether any given meeting is booked.

The rule

There is no partial credit. Six out of seven is an unqualified meeting with a good excuse.

Binary judgements can be made consistently by different people on different days. Graded ones cannot, and a standard that cannot be applied consistently is not a standard. Each criterion has a formal name and a plain-language name. They mean the same thing, and either may be cited.

CRITERION 01

Role

Right person

The attendee holds a role with documented decision-making authority, or direct functional responsibility, in the category being sold into.

Assessed on the role itself, not on the person's claims about budget or seniority.

Passes

Head of Revenue Operations attends a meeting about sales tooling. VP Engineering attends a meeting about developer infrastructure. A procurement manager attends a vendor evaluation in their category.

Fails

A junior analyst with no stated remit takes the call because their director forwarded the email. An office manager attends a meeting about enterprise data governance. A founder attends but has explicitly delegated purchasing authority to someone who is not present.

What "documented" means. A title verifiable against LinkedIn, the company website, an org chart, an email signature or a company directory. Where the company has published a role definition or org structure, that definition governs. Where the role is ambiguous or undisclosed, the attendee must state their authority in the meeting itself, and that statement is recorded.

Deliberately excluded — budget and purchase timing

A prospect saying "we have budget for this" or "we'll be buying in Q2" on a first call is a claim about the future, not a fact, and cannot be verified by anyone. Establishing budget and timing is the closer's job, not the setter's. Holding a setter to an unverifiable self-report makes a standard both unfair and unrepeatable.

Where a campaign has a legitimate timing requirement, it is written into the profile at campaign start and tested under criterion 2.

EVIDENCE · attendee name, verified job title, and the company's own listing of that role. Any clarification of authority given during the meeting is recorded in the meeting report.

CRITERION 02

Fit

Right company

The account matches the Ideal Customer Profile agreed in writing before outreach began.

The ICP must be recorded at campaign start and cannot be changed retrospectively. A buyer who broadens their ICP mid-campaign cannot reject earlier meetings against the new definition; an agency cannot claim an out-of-profile account was "close enough".

Passes

ICP states UK-headquartered SaaS businesses, 50–500 employees. Account is a 200-person UK SaaS business.

Fails

ICP states 50–500 employees. Account has 12. The buyer's ICP was vague, so the agency interpreted it generously.

Timing requirements live here

A buyer may write a timing condition into the profile — contract renews before June, currently in an RFP, has posted a role for this function — and where they have, it is tested under this criterion like any other attribute, against the record rather than against what the prospect said they intended.

What cannot be tested is a prospect's statement about when they expect to buy. The distinction is not what the condition is about, but whether it was agreed in writing before outreach began and can be checked against something other than the prospect's own forecast.

If a campaign's ICP is too loose to adjudicate against, that is a finding in itself, and the honest answer is that the campaign should not have launched.

EVIDENCE · the written ICP from campaign setup, plus the account's checkable attributes.

CRITERION 03

Awareness

They knew who they were meeting

The attendee could state, in their own words, what the vendor does — before or at the start of the meeting.

The test is whether a real person knowingly agreed to talk to a specific company about a specific thing. Not whether they were impressed.

Passes

"You're the compliance monitoring people — Sarah mentioned you handle the FCA reporting side."

Fails

"Sorry, remind me who you are again? I just accept most of these."

Recognition, not agreement

Whether the attendee understood they were entering a commercial conversation is criterion 5. The two are separated because they fail for different reasons and say different things about an agency: a prospect can know exactly who the vendor is and still have been misled about what the meeting was for, and a prospect can have consented to a sales call in general terms without knowing which company was on the other end.

EVIDENCE · the meeting report records what the attendee understood the vendor to do. Where the attendee could not articulate it, that is recorded plainly.

CRITERION 04

Need

They said it themselves

The attendee described a problem, situation or objective that falls within the vendor's solution space.

The need does not have to be urgent, funded, or prioritised. It has to be real and relevant. "We're fine, but tell me more" is not a need. "Our renewals process is manual and it's becoming a problem" is.

Passes

Prospect describes a current manual process the vendor automates.

Fails

Prospect took the call as a favour, has no relevant problem, and says so.

Note the asymmetry with criterion 1

Need is assessed on what the prospect actually said about their present situation, which is observable. Budget and purchase timing are assessed on what they claim about the future, which is not. That is why one is in the Standard and the others are not.

EVIDENCE · the need as described, in the attendee's own framing, recorded in the meeting report.

CRITERION 05

Consent

They agreed to a sales call

The attendee knowingly agreed to a sales conversation with the vendor.

They understood they were booking a commercial discussion. They were not misled about the nature, subject or host of the meeting.

Passes

Prospect replied to outreach, agreed to a call about the vendor's product, and a calendar invite named the vendor.

Fails

The meeting was secured by offering a gift card. The invitation described "a short research interview" or "an industry benchmarking chat" and became a pitch. The prospect believed they were meeting a different company.

This protects the buyer's brand, not just their invoice

A meeting obtained by misdirection is a liability even when someone attends, and an industry standard that permitted it would be worthless.

EVIDENCE · the booking mechanism, the wording of the invitation, and any incentive offered.

CRITERION 06

Attendance

They turned up

The meeting took place, with the qualifying attendee present, for long enough to hold a real conversation.

Passes

Meeting occurred; the qualifying attendee joined and participated.

Fails

No-show. Attendee joined for two minutes and left. Only a delegate attended, and that delegate does not independently satisfy criterion 1.

Rescheduling and no-shows

A meeting rebooked before its start time is not a failure. It carries its original submission forward. Up to two reschedules are permitted; beyond that the meeting is void and not counted either way.

A no-show fails this criterion. If the same attendee is rebooked and attends within 14 days, the meeting may be resubmitted once as a new meeting.

EVIDENCE · attendance confirmed by the vendor, and independently by the attendee where they respond to a confirmation request. A non-response from the attendee is recorded but does not by itself fail the criterion.

CRITERION 07

Novelty

New to the buyer

The account was not already in the buyer's active pipeline, and was not on the suppression list supplied at campaign start.

The buyer must supply a suppression list before outreach begins. An account absent from that list, and not independently in an active sales process within the prior 90 days, is novel.

Passes

Account has no prior contact with the buyer, or last contact was over 90 days ago and closed.

Fails

Account was in an open opportunity. Account was on the suppression list. The same account already produced a meeting on this campaign within 90 days.

The obligation runs both ways

A buyer who fails to supply a suppression list cannot later reject meetings on novelty grounds.

EVIDENCE · the suppression list from campaign setup, plus the buyer's CRM record.

Rules of application

How the seven are used once money is involved.

Objection window
A held meeting is Qualified unless the buyer flags it within three business days, counted in the buyer's own timezone. Silence clears the meeting.
Burden
The agency evidences the meeting. The buyer evidences any objection, against a named criterion. "It felt weak" is not an objection; "criterion 2, the account has 15 employees against an ICP floor of 50" is.
Adjudication
Each criterion is decided separately. A single failed criterion fails the meeting, but the record shows which one — because "failed on Fit" and "failed on Consent" are very different findings about an agency.
Versioning
Every verdict is recorded against the Standard version in force when the meeting occurred. Later versions never apply retrospectively.
Amendment
Changes are published with a rationale and a comment period. The Standard improves by being argued with.
What it does not do
It does not measure revenue, or whether a meeting was good, or budget and purchase intent. A standard that tried to measure all of those would measure none of them reliably.

The Millesimal Rating

Cleared meetings per thousand submitted. Arithmetic, not opinion.

925STERLING

The name comes from hallmarking. Before precious metal can be sold in Britain, an independent Assay Office tests it and stamps its purity as a number out of 1,000 — sterling silver is 925. Buyers trust the stamp rather than the seller's word. That is a several-hundred-year-old answer to exactly the problem this industry has.

An agency at 870 delivers 870 qualified meetings for every 1,000 it books. Because it is arithmetic rather than taste, it can be published without becoming a matter of opinion.

For context, industry sources put healthy B2B show rates at 70–80%. A rating is a stricter measure than show rate, since attendance is only one of seven criteria — so ratings should be expected to sit below an agency's show rate, and the gap between the two is itself informative.

Adopting it

Copy it. Argue with it. Both are the point.

Licence

Free to copy, cite and adopt. No permission required. Cite it as the Millesimal Standard v1.0 in a contract, or copy the seven criteria into your own terms. Attribution is appreciated, not required.

One restriction, and only one: no modified version may be published under the Millesimal name, or described as a version of the Millesimal Standard. Change it freely for your own use — but a document that says "Millesimal" has to be this document, or the name stops meaning anything.

Agreeing a definition before outreach starts is worth more than which definition you agree. If a criterion is wrong, unfair or unworkable in your market, say so — that is how version 1.1 gets written.

The clause, for a contract

Qualified Meeting. A meeting is a Qualified Meeting if and only if it satisfies all seven criteria of the Millesimal Standard v1.0, as published at millesimal.uk. The version in force at the date of the meeting governs; later versions do not apply retrospectively. A held meeting is deemed Qualified unless the Buyer notifies the Agency of an objection, identifying the specific criterion failed and supplying supporting evidence, within three (3) working days of the meeting.
