Why this exists

The term was left undefined until money was at stake.

Every outbound engagement in B2B rests on a phrase almost nobody defines precisely: a qualified meeting. Buyers pay per meeting. Agencies bill per meeting. And when the invoice arrives, the two parties discover they meant different things.

The argument that follows is not a failure of goodwill. It is the predictable result of a commercial term being left open until the money is already on the table.

This document is one attempt at a definition both sides could accept in advance. It is deliberately public, deliberately versioned, and deliberately written by a party with no stake in whether any given meeting gets booked. You may adopt it, adapt it, or argue with it. All three are useful.

The rule

A meeting is Qualified if, and only if, it satisfies all seven criteria below.

There is no partial credit. Six out of seven is not a qualified meeting; it is an unqualified meeting with a good excuse. Binary judgments can be made consistently by different people on different days. Graded ones cannot — and a standard that cannot be applied consistently is not a standard.

01

Role

right person #c1

The attendee holds a role with documented decision-making authority, or direct functional responsibility, in the category being sold into.

Assessed on the role itself, not on the person’s claims about budget or seniority.

Passes
Head of Revenue Operations attends a meeting about sales tooling. VP Engineering attends a meeting about developer infrastructure. A procurement manager attends a vendor evaluation in their own category.
Fails
A junior analyst with no stated remit takes the call because their director forwarded the email. An office manager attends a meeting about enterprise data governance. A founder attends but has explicitly delegated purchasing to someone who is not present.

What “documented” means. A title verifiable against LinkedIn, the company website, an org chart, an email signature or a company directory. Where the company has published a role definition, that definition governs. Where the role is ambiguous or undisclosed, the attendee must state their authority in the meeting itself, and that statement is recorded.

Deliberately excluded: budget and purchase timing. A prospect saying “we have budget” or “we’ll be buying in Q2” on a first call is a claim about the future, not a fact, and cannot be verified by anyone. Establishing budget and timing is the closer’s job, not the setter’s. Where a campaign has a legitimate timing requirement, it is written into the profile at campaign start and tested under criterion 2.

Evidence: attendee name, verified job title, and the company’s own listing of that role.

02

Fit

right company #c2

The account matches the Ideal Customer Profile agreed in writing before outreach began.

The ICP must be recorded at campaign start and cannot be changed retrospectively. A buyer who broadens their ICP mid-campaign cannot reject earlier meetings against the new definition; an agency cannot claim an out-of-profile account was “close enough”.

Passes
ICP states UK-headquartered SaaS businesses, 50–500 employees. The account is a 200-person UK SaaS business.
Fails
ICP states 50–500 employees. The account has 12. The buyer’s ICP was vague, so the agency interpreted it generously.

Timing requirements live here. A buyer may write a timing condition into the profile — contract renews before June, currently in an RFP, has posted a role for this function — and where they have, it is tested under this criterion like any other attribute, against the record rather than against what the prospect said they intended. The distinction is not what the condition is about, but whether it was agreed in writing before outreach began and can be checked against something other than the prospect’s own forecast.

If a campaign’s ICP is too loose to adjudicate against, that is a finding in itself, and the honest answer is that the campaign should not have launched.

Evidence: the written ICP from campaign setup, plus the account’s checkable attributes.

03

Awareness

they knew who they were meeting #c3

The attendee could state, in their own words, what the vendor does — before or at the start of the meeting.

The test is whether a real person knowingly agreed to talk to a specific company about a specific thing. Not whether they were impressed.

Passes
“You’re the compliance monitoring people — Sarah mentioned you handle the FCA reporting side.”
Fails
“Sorry, remind me who you are again? I just accept most of these.”

This is about recognition, not agreement. Whether the attendee understood they were entering a commercial conversation is criterion 5. The two are separated because they fail for different reasons and say different things about an agency: a prospect can know exactly who the vendor is and still have been misled about what the meeting was for.

Evidence: the meeting report records what the attendee understood the vendor to do. Where they could not articulate it, that is recorded plainly.

04

Need

they said it themselves #c4

The attendee described a problem, situation or objective that falls within the vendor’s solution space.

The need does not have to be urgent, funded, or prioritised. It has to be real and relevant. “We’re fine, but tell me more” is not a need. “Our renewals process is manual and it’s becoming a problem” is.

Passes
The prospect describes a current manual process the vendor automates.
Fails
The prospect took the call as a favour, has no relevant problem, and says so.

Note the asymmetry with criterion 1. Need is assessed on what the prospect actually said about their present situation, which is observable. Budget and purchase timing are assessed on what they claim about the future, which is not. That is why one is in the Standard and the others are not.

Evidence: the need as described, in the attendee’s own framing, recorded in the meeting report.

05

Consent

they agreed to a sales call #c5

The attendee knowingly agreed to a sales conversation with the vendor.

They understood they were booking a commercial discussion. They were not misled about the nature, subject or host of the meeting.

Passes
The prospect replied to outreach, agreed to a call about the vendor’s product, and a calendar invitation named the vendor.
Fails
The meeting was secured by offering a gift card. The invitation described “a short research interview” or “an industry benchmarking chat” and became a pitch. The prospect believed they were meeting a different company.

This criterion protects the buyer’s brand, not just their invoice. A meeting obtained by misdirection is a liability even when someone attends, and an industry standard that permitted it would be worthless.

Evidence: the booking mechanism, the wording of the invitation, and any incentive offered.

06

Attendance

they turned up #c6

The meeting took place, with the qualifying attendee present, for long enough to hold a real conversation.

Passes
The meeting occurred; the qualifying attendee joined and participated.
Fails
No-show. The attendee joined for two minutes and left. Only a delegate attended, and that delegate does not independently satisfy criterion 1.

Rescheduling. A meeting rebooked before its start time is not a failure and carries its original submission forward. Up to two reschedules are permitted; beyond that the meeting is void and counted neither way.

No-shows. A no-show fails this criterion. If the same attendee is rebooked and attends within 14 days, the meeting may be resubmitted once as a new meeting.

Evidence: attendance confirmed by the vendor, and independently by the attendee where they respond. A non-response is recorded but does not by itself fail the criterion.

07

Novelty

new to the buyer #c7

The account was not already in the buyer’s active pipeline, and was not on the suppression list supplied at campaign start.

The buyer must supply a suppression list before outreach begins. An account absent from that list, and not independently in an active sales process within the prior 90 days, is novel.

Passes
The account has no prior contact with the buyer, or last contact was over 90 days ago and closed.
Fails
The account was in an open opportunity. The account was on the suppression list. The same account already produced a meeting on this campaign within 90 days.

A buyer who fails to supply a suppression list cannot later reject meetings on novelty grounds. The obligation runs both ways.

Evidence: the suppression list from campaign setup, plus the buyer’s CRM record.

Rules of application

How the criteria are used when the two sides disagree.

RuleWhat it says
Objection windowA held meeting is Qualified unless the buyer flags it within three business days, counted in the buyer’s own timezone. Silence clears the meeting.
BurdenThe agency evidences the meeting. The buyer evidences any objection, against a named criterion. “It felt weak” is not an objection; “criterion 2, the account has 15 employees against an ICP floor of 50” is.
AdjudicationEach criterion is decided separately. A single failed criterion fails the meeting, but the record shows which one — because “failed on Fit” and “failed on Consent” are very different findings about an agency.
Contemporaneous recordWhere accounts conflict, the contemporaneous artefact is preferred over either party’s later characterisation of it.
Held overA genuinely borderline criterion may be held to a second sitting a week later rather than decided the same day. No provisional view is recorded at the first sitting.
VersioningEvery ruling is recorded against the Standard version in force when the meeting occurred. Later versions never apply retrospectively.
AmendmentChanges are published with a rationale and a comment period. The Standard improves by being argued with.
RecordingsNo call recordings or transcripts are accepted, at any time, for any reason. Rulings are made on the written record both parties agreed to produce.
Versioning

No version is ever applied to a meeting that happened before it existed.

ChangeWhat it means
1.0.1Wording clarified. Nothing that passed now fails, and nothing that failed now passes.
1.1A criterion’s threshold, evidence or scope changes. Published in draft with a rationale and not less than 30 days for argument before it comes into force.
2.0A criterion is added or removed, or the all-or-nothing rule itself changes.

Superseded versions stay published at their own addresses, so a ruling issued years ago can still be read against the exact text it was decided under. The full history — including the arguments currently open against v1.0 — is kept in the repository changelog.

The Millesimal Rating

Qualified meetings per thousand submitted.

An agency at 870 delivers 870 qualified meetings for every thousand it books. It is arithmetic, not opinion — which is why it can be published without it being a matter of taste.

The name comes from hallmarking. Before precious metal can be sold in Britain, an independent Assay Office tests it and stamps its purity as a number out of a thousand; sterling silver is 925. Buyers trust the stamp rather than the seller’s word. That is a several-hundred-year-old answer to exactly the problem this industry has.

Industry sources put healthy B2B show rates at 70–80%. A rating is stricter than a show rate, since attendance is only one of seven criteria — so ratings should be expected to sit below an agency’s show rate, and the gap between the two is itself informative.

What this Standard does not do
—
It does not measure whether a meeting produced revenue.That depends on the vendor’s sales execution, not the setter’s work.
—
It does not measure whether a meeting was good.A qualified meeting can still go badly.
—
It does not assess budget or purchase intent.Those are the closer’s job. A timing condition agreed in writing before outreach is a different thing, and is tested under criterion 2.
—
It does not rank agencies on anything else.Only the proportion of submitted meetings that met the definition.

A standard that tried to measure all of these would measure none of them reliably.

Adopting it

Take it. You do not need us to use it.

Cite it as the Millesimal Standard v1.0 in a contract, or copy the seven criteria into your own terms. Attribution appreciated, not required.

Agreeing a definition before outreach starts is worth more than which definition you agree. If a criterion is wrong, unfair or unworkable in your market, say so — that is how version 1.1 gets written, and the arguments that change it are published alongside it.

Contract clause
A meeting is a Qualified Meeting for the purposes of this agreement if, and only if, it satisfies all seven criteria of the Millesimal Standard v1.0 (Role, Fit, Awareness, Need, Consent, Attendance, Novelty), as published at millesimal.uk/standard. The Ideal Customer Profile and the suppression list shall be agreed in writing before outreach begins and shall not be varied retrospectively. A Qualified Meeting is deemed accepted unless the Buyer contests it, against a named criterion, within three (3) business days of the meeting. Where the parties do not agree, either may refer the meeting for independent determination against the version of the Standard in force on the date the meeting occurred.
