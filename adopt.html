The argument

A rating is the only claim a buyer cannot discount.

01

A case study is a selected sample

Every agency has three good logos and a slide about them. A buyer knows the sample was chosen by the seller, so they discount it to nearly nothing — and they are right to.

02

A rating is a sample you did not choose

Twenty meetings drawn by someone else, across at least two campaigns and two months, against a definition published before the audit. That is the entire difference.

03

It moves the conversation off rate

When quality is unmeasurable, price is the only axis left. A number out of a thousand gives a careful agency something to charge for — and gives the buyer a reason to pay it.

Expect your rating to sit below your show rate. Attendance is only one of seven criteria, and a rating is a stricter measure by construction. The gap between the two is itself the interesting number — and explaining it is a better conversation than defending a show rate.
Certification

Audited, renewed, and withdrawable.

An audited statement of the proportion of your submitted meetings that met the Standard over a defined period. It is not an endorsement, not a recommendation, and not a guarantee of future performance — and you may not describe it as any of those.

What certification costs you, stated plainly.

An agency that only hears the upside makes a decision it regrets at renewal. These are the four things worth being sure about before you sign.

01
Your first number may be lower than you expect.Seven binary checks with no partial credit is stricter than any internal QA process. Most agencies have never been measured this way.
02
The historic rating persists after you leave.Withdrawing does not erase it. It is marked as historic and stays. That is what makes a live rating worth anything.
03
Falsified evidence is published.The one exception to consent-based publication. It has to be, or the rest of the scheme is theatre.

Before you pay anyone

Put the definition in the contract first.

The single highest-return thing an agency can do here costs nothing: agree what a qualified meeting is, in writing, before outreach starts — at the moment when neither side knows which way any particular meeting will go.

Most of the arguments this business exists to settle would not have happened if the definition had been agreed in advance. That is not a great commercial position for us. It is true anyway.

Three things to fix this week

Record the ICP at campaign start

In writing, dated, with the employee band and any timing condition stated as a checkable attribute. An ICP too loose to adjudicate against is a campaign that should not have launched.

Demand the suppression list

A buyer who supplies no list waives objections on criterion 7 entirely. Getting it is free and removes your most common dispute.

Name the vendor in the invitation

Criterion 5 turns on the artefact the attendee acted on. An invitation that names the vendor and the commercial nature of the meeting survives almost any later characterisation of the first approach.

First five agencies get the founding rate.

Held for three years, named on the site, and priced as compensation for adopting a standard nobody has heard of yet.
