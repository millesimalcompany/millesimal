Published position

Saying we are independent is worth nothing.

So here is what would have to be true instead. Each commitment below is stated as a way Millesimal could be bought, paired with the thing that prevents it. They are published so that they can be held against us.

No commission, ever Refusals logged with reasons No recordings accepted
01

Paid the same either way.

The price does not vary with the outcome, the size of the account, the size of the invoice, or who is asking. A flat fee per meeting, published, identical whether the ruling clears the meeting or kills it.

Stops · selling the outcome
02

We refuse work where a conflict exists, and log the refusal with its reason.

No commercial relationship with either side. No meeting we introduced. No adjudication on one-sided evidence. Refusals are recorded, with the reason, so the pattern of what we turn down is itself checkable.

Stops · the quiet favour
03

Both sides get the same document, at the same time, with reasoning per criterion.

There is no private version, no summary for one side and detail for the other, and no covering note that says something the ruling does not.

Stops · two versions of the truth
04

Either side may reply on the record within fourteen days.

A reply does not change the ruling. It is recorded alongside it and published wherever the ruling is published, for as long as the ruling exists. A specimen on this site carries one — including a buyer saying they still disagree.

Stops · the unanswerable verdict
05

Rulings are never edited.

Where a material error is established, a superseding ruling is issued naming the original. Both stay up. A record that can be quietly tidied is a marketing asset, not evidence.

Stops · the rewrite
06

We do not book meetings, sell leads, take referral fees or earn commission.

The ruling is the only product. There is no second revenue line whose health depends on meetings being cleared, and no introduction anywhere in the business that could later need protecting.

Stops · the structural conflict
07

An agency’s score is never published without its written consent.

This one is a cost, not a virtue, and it is published with its downside admitted rather than dressed up. A body that published ratings without consent would receive no meetings, and a standard with nothing to measure measures nothing. The obvious attack — so bad scores stay hidden — is correct, and stating the trade-off is stronger than hiding it.

The honest cost of getting supply
08

The method is public.

Each check is decided in isolation. The reasoning for each is written before the next is read, and before the overall result is known — so the result cannot work backwards into the reasons.

Stops · the result deciding the reasons
Disclosure

One adjudicator, declared to you before you submit anything.

Millesimal is currently a single adjudicator. Any party to a ruling is told who that is, and what else they do, before they send a single piece of evidence — and either side may refuse that adjudicator on those grounds, at no cost and without giving a reason.

That disclosure goes to the parties, not to the internet. It is how disclosure works everywhere it carries weight: arbitral bodies do not publish an arbitrator’s CV, they declare position to the parties and grant a right of objection before proceedings begin. The people with money at stake get the facts and a veto.

Automatic refusals, with no discretion: no ruling on any meeting involving the adjudicator’s other employer, its customers, or its competitors. That sits under commitment 02.

The limitation, admitted

One person deciding is a real weakness. It is stated here rather than hidden, with the mitigations published alongside it:

  • Each check decided separately, reasoning written before the next is read.
  • Genuinely borderline calls wait a week for a second sitting rather than a coin flip.
  • A second reader before issue once volume allows — and that change will be stated on this page when it happens.
No recordings, ever. No call recordings or transcripts are accepted at any time, for any reason. Rulings are made on the written record both parties agreed to produce — which is also the only class of evidence a prospect never consented to have judged.
Version 1.1

The review panel exists to disagree with us in public.

Named practitioners, credited on the published Standard, free to argue against any criterion in public. The arguments that change the Standard are published alongside it, with their rationale and a comment period.

There is no cost, no commitment to adopt, and no requirement to be nice about it. A standard that only its author defends is not a standard — it is a pricing page with footnotes.
