01. What goes wrong

Paying per meeting does not end the argument. It moves it.

Pay a monthly fee, you argue about effort once a quarter. Pay per meeting, you argue about every invoice.

You want fewer meetings to count. The agency wants more to count. Neither of you is neutral. Both of you are working from the same thin evidence. So the argument comes back every month.

01

There is barely any evidence

A meeting leaves behind a diary entry and two memories. You cannot prove the prospect was wrong for you. The agency cannot prove they were right. So nobody wins the argument and it comes back next month.

02

People do not turn up

Some booked meetings never happen. Most contracts cover that in one vague sentence. So every month you are quietly paying more per real meeting than you think.

03

You cannot shop on quality

When you pick an agency you get case studies and their own numbers. A good agency and a bad agency look identical. That is exactly when the cheap one wins.

A whole industry sells something nobody neutral defines, nobody neutral counts, nobody neutral records.

02. How it works

Five steps. Nothing to install.

You send one meeting in. We ask the prospect a single question, run the seven checks against what both sides agreed before the campaign started, and give you a written ruling you can put in front of the agency.

STEP 02

Did this meeting happen, and did you know who you were meeting? One email. No recordings are accepted, at any time, for any reason.

STEP 03

Each one a yes or a no, tested against the brief both sides signed before outreach began — not against how anyone feels now.

STEP 05

It counts toward the agency's score whether it passed or failed. Neither side can delete it, and it outlives the contract.

03. The 7 checks

A meeting passes all 7 checks or it does not count.

Every check is a yes or a no. There is no half mark. Half marks feel fairer but two people never score them the same way.

These are the seven criteria of the Millesimal Standard v1.0, in plain words. Each check below names the criterion it comes from, so a ruling can be cited against the published document.

04. The score

Every ruling is kept. That is the part nobody can copy.

Count 1,000 meetings an agency sends in. Count how many passed. That number is its score.

It is a sum, not an opinion. Nobody hands out marks. The score is simply what the rulings add up to.

925Sterling

Where the scale comes from

Before silver can be sold in Britain, an independent assay office tests it and stamps its purity as a number out of a thousand. Sterling is 925 parts silver per 1,000. The buyer trusts the stamp, not the seller's word.

A Millesimal Rating is the same idea and the same scale. 870 means 870 qualified meetings for every 1,000 booked — checked by us, not claimed by them.

06. Independence

Saying we are independent is worth nothing. These are the rules that make it checkable.

Every one of these is a way we could be bought, and the thing that stops it. If we ever break one, you will be able to point at the line.

01 We are paid the same either way.

A ruling costs the same whether the meeting passes or fails. The price does not move with the size of the account, the size of the invoice, or who is asking. There is no version of this where we earn more by ruling one way.

Stops us selling the outcome
02 We turn down work where we are conflicted.

We will not rule where we have any commercial relationship with either side beyond the ruling itself, where we introduced the two parties, or where only one side has been given a chance to put evidence in. Every refusal is logged with its reason.

Stops the quiet favour
03 Both sides get the same document.

One ruling, issued to both parties at the same time, with the reasoning for each of the seven checks written out. Nothing is said to one side that is not said to the other, and there is no private version.

Stops two versions of the truth
04 Either side can answer back, on the record.

Fourteen days to submit a written response. It does not change the ruling. It is kept with it and shown wherever the ruling is shown, for as long as the ruling exists.

Stops the unanswerable verdict
05 Rulings are never edited.

If we get one wrong, we issue a new one that names the original and says what changed. Both stay on the record permanently. Nothing is ever quietly corrected, and nothing is ever taken down.

Stops the rewrite
06 We do not book meetings or take a cut of any.

No referral fees, no marketplace, no commission, no reselling. The ruling is the only thing we sell. Every other business in this market makes more money when more meetings pass.

Stops the structural conflict
07 A score belongs to the agency that earned it.

We never publish an individual agency's score without its written say-so. Aggregate figures across the market, yes. Naming and shaming, no — because a body that publishes scores without consent gets sent no meetings, and a standard with nothing to measure measures nothing.

The honest cost of getting supply
08 The method is public, so you can check our working.

Each check is decided on its own, in order, with the reason written down before the next one is looked at and before the overall result is known. If a ruling's reasoning mentions whether the deal went anywhere, it is wrong and gets done again.

Automatic refusals

We will not rule on a meeting involving that employer, its customers, or its competitors. Those fall under rule 02 above, and there is no discretion in it.

The limitation, stated plainly

One person deciding is a real weakness, and pretending otherwise would be the first dishonest thing on this page. What we do about it: each check is decided separately with its reasoning written before the next one is read, and anything genuinely too close to call waits a week for a second sitting rather than a coin flip. As volume grows, rulings get a second reader before issue — and when that changes it will be said here.
