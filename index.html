02 · Why the argument happens

Paying per meeting does not end the argument. It starts it.

01

Nobody defined the word

Every outbound contract in B2B rests on the phrase a qualified meeting. Almost none of them define it. The disagreement is not a failure of goodwill — it is what happens when a commercial term is left open until the invoice lands.

02

Both judges are paid to disagree

The buyer decides whether to pay. The agency decides whether to argue. Each of them is the referee of a match they have a stake in, which is the one arrangement no other industry accepts.

03

So the good agencies lose too

With no way to tell a careful setter from a careless one, buyers price every agency as if it were the careless one. Being good at this is currently unprovable, and therefore unpaid.

03 · The definition

A meeting passes all seven checks, or it is not a qualified meeting.

There is no partial credit. Six out of seven is not a qualified meeting; it is an unqualified meeting with a good excuse. Binary judgments can be made the same way by different people on different days — graded ones cannot, and a standard that cannot be applied consistently is not a standard.

05 · The rating

An agency at 870 clears 870 meetings in every thousand it books.

That is arithmetic, not taste — which is why it can be published without anybody having to agree about what “good” means. It is the oldest working answer to exactly this problem, and this industry has never had one.

A rating is stricter than a show rate, because attendance is only one of the seven checks. Expect ratings to sit below an agency’s show rate. The gap between the two is itself the interesting number.

06 · When a meeting is contested

Five steps. Two business days. One document.

You send the meeting

One disputed meeting, the criterion you are objecting under, and the written record you already have. No new paperwork is invented for this.

We tell you who is deciding

Before either side submits a single piece of evidence, both parties are told who the adjudicator is and what else they do. Either side may refuse them, at no cost, without giving a reason.

Both sides file

The agency evidences the meeting. The buyer evidences the objection, against a named criterion. No call recordings or transcripts are accepted — ever, for any reason.

Each check is decided alone

Seven separate decisions, the reasoning for each written before the next is read and before the overall result is known. A borderline call waits a week for a second sitting rather than a coin flip.

One document, both sides, same moment

Per-criterion reasoning, what would have changed the answer, and fourteen days for either side to reply on the record. There is no private version.

The part people miss

Every ruling is kept.

Rulings are never edited. Where a material error is established, a superseding ruling is issued naming the original — and both stay up. A reply filed by either party is published wherever the ruling is published, for as long as the ruling exists.

An append-only record is the only kind that is worth anything. A record that can be quietly tidied is a marketing asset, not evidence.

Window
Three business days to contest. Silence clears the meeting.
Burden
The objector evidences the objection. “It felt weak” is not an objection.
Version
Every ruling binds to the Standard in force on the day of the meeting.

Declared to you, not to the internet

One adjudicator, named to you before you submit anything.

Millesimal is currently one adjudicator. Any party to a ruling is told who that is, and what else they do, before they send a single piece of evidence — and either side may refuse that adjudicator on those grounds, at no cost and without giving a reason.

One person deciding is a real weakness, and it is stated here rather than hidden. The mitigations are published: each check decided separately, reasoning written before the next is read, borderline calls held over to a second sitting a week later, and a second reader before issue once volume allows — a change that will be announced on this page when it happens.

Note on 07. A body that published ratings without consent would receive no meetings, and a standard with nothing to measure measures nothing. That trade-off is worth stating with its downside admitted rather than dressed up as a principle.

08 · Use it without us

The most useful thing here is free.

Agreeing a definition before outreach starts is worth more than which definition you agree. Put this clause in the contract and most of these arguments stop happening — whether or not anyone ever pays us for a ruling.

No permission required, no attribution required, no licence to accept. If a criterion is wrong or unworkable in your market, say so — that is how version 1.1 gets written.

A meeting is a Qualified Meeting for the purposes of this agreement if, and only if, it satisfies all seven criteria of the Millesimal Standard v1.0 (Role, Fit, Awareness, Need, Consent, Attendance, Novelty), as published at millesimal.uk/standard. The Ideal Customer Profile and the suppression list shall be agreed in writing before outreach begins and shall not be varied retrospectively. A Qualified Meeting is deemed accepted unless the Buyer contests it, against a named criterion, within three (3) business days of the meeting. Where the parties do not agree, either may refer the meeting for independent determination against the version of the Standard in force on the date the meeting occurred.
