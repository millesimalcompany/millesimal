02 · Why meetings get disputed

Paying per meeting leads to arguments about which meetings count.

01

Nobody defines “qualified”

B2B outbound contracts rest on the phrase a qualified meeting, yet almost none define it. So the argument starts when the invoice arrives.

02

Each side judges its own case

The buyer decides whether to pay and the agency decides whether to argue. Each referees a match it has a stake in.

03

Good agencies lose out too

Buyers cannot tell careful setters from careless ones, so they price every agency as careless. Good work goes unproven and unpaid.

05 · The rating

An agency rated 870 clears 870 of every 1,000 meetings it submits.

It is a count, so nobody has to agree what “good” means. Hallmarking has worked this way for seven centuries. Outbound sales has never had it.

Expect a rating to sit below an agency’s show rate, because attendance is only one of seven checks.

06 · When a meeting is contested

Five steps to one written ruling, in two business days.

You send the meeting

Send the disputed meeting, the criterion you object under, and the written record you already have. No new paperwork.

We tell you who is deciding

Before any evidence is sent, both sides learn who the adjudicator is and what else they do. Either side can refuse them, free and without giving a reason.

Both sides file

The agency sends evidence for the meeting. The buyer sends evidence for the objection, under a named criterion. We never accept call recordings or transcripts.

Each check is decided alone

Its reasoning is written before the next check is read and before the result is known. Borderline calls wait a week for a second sitting.

Both sides get the ruling at once

It gives the reasoning for each criterion and what would have changed the answer. Either side has fourteen days to reply on the record. There is no private version.

The part people miss

Every ruling is kept.

Rulings are never edited. A material error gets a new ruling that names the original, and both stay on the record. Replies are kept with the ruling.

Window
Three business days to contest. Silence clears the meeting.
Burden
Whoever objects must prove it. “It felt weak” is not an objection.
Version
Each ruling applies the Standard in force on the day of the meeting.

Use the Standard in your contracts for free.

Contract clause · copy & paste
A meeting is a Qualified Meeting for the purposes of this agreement if, and only if, it satisfies all seven criteria of the Millesimal Standard v1.0 (Role, Fit, Awareness, Need, Consent, Attendance, Novelty), as published at millesimal.uk/standard. The Ideal Customer Profile and the suppression list shall be agreed in writing before outreach begins and shall not be varied retrospectively. A Qualified Meeting is deemed accepted unless the Buyer contests it, against a named criterion, within three (3) business days of the meeting. Where the parties do not agree, either may refer the meeting for independent determination against the version of the Standard in force on the date the meeting occurred.
