How to read one

The six parts of a ruling.

Each part is written so that a sceptical reader who was not there can check the reasoning.

Determination
Qualified or not qualified. There is no grade or score.
Criterion table
One decision for each criterion, with the evidence relied on. Contested criteria are marked, even if the objection failed.
The failed check
Where a criterion failed: what the losing side argued, and why it fails.
Counterfactual
What would have changed the outcome. This makes the ruling testable and tells a losing party what to fix.
Scope note
What is not decided: anyone’s conduct, the quality of the conversation or the commercial outcome.
Right of reply
Either side has fourteen days to respond on the record. The reply does not change the ruling but is always published with it.
The specimens

Why we chose these three.

0031 rules against an agency. 0044 rules against the party who paid, which makes 0031 credible. 0057 shows the method on a hard case.

Six criteria met, one failed. The meeting does not count.

The account had 14 employees. The ICP floor, agreed in writing at campaign start, was 50. The agency argued the business was growing fast and close enough.

That argument fails. Size can be checked, and the account is below the floor. An ICP that could be met approximately could not be ruled on.

This says nothing about the quality of the meeting. Criterion 1, Role, passed. Only the failed criterion is named.

The buyer’s objection had no evidence. The meeting stands.

The buyer said their own team had contacted the account earlier in the year. They produced no CRM record, and the account was not on their suppression list.

The buyer may be right. But a criterion cannot fail on a claim nobody can evidence. Under rule R2, the objector must prove the objection.

A body that only ruled against agencies would just be a refund service for buyers.

What would have changed it: a CRM record of an open or recently closed opportunity, or the account on the suppression list. Either would have failed criterion 7.

A close call, decided at a second sitting a week later.

The first message offered a 20 minute “benchmarking conversation”. The calendar invitation the attendee accepted named the vendor and called it an introduction. That needed more than the usual fifteen minutes to decide.

Under rule R6 it went to a second sitting a week later. The first sitting recorded no view, so the second could not just agree with it. Under R3 the invitation decided it: it is what the attendee acted on.

The ruling states: “This verdict clears this invitation only. It does not clear outreach framed as research in general.” A ruling usable as a general licence would be worth nothing.

The buyer’s reply. Filed within fourteen days, it accepts the reasoning on the invitation but says “the first message was misleading”. The agency has agreed to stop using a research framing on this campaign. The reply is recorded with no further finding and published with the ruling.

Scope

What a ruling does not do.

×
Not arbitration.An expert determination on the evidence supplied. It does not settle contract disputes.
×
Not a finding about conduct.Nobody is found at fault. A criterion was either met on the record or not.
×
Not a judgement on the conversation.A qualified meeting can still go badly. That is for the vendor.
×
Never edited.A proven material error leads to a new ruling that names the original. Both stay on the record.

Both parties choose binding or advisory when they sign. Advisory is the default. Binding is required when a fee is held in escrow.
