How we are paid

A flat fee, agreed before anyone files anything.

The fee is the same whichever way the ruling goes. It does not vary with the outcome, the size of the account, the size of the invoice, or who is asking. That is the first of the eight published commitments, and it is the one that makes the rest of them possible.

It is deliberately set below the value at stake. A disputed meeting has already cost you the fee you paid for it, and — if it was wrongly accepted — an AE’s time to re-qualify it. An adjudication that cost more than the argument would not be worth buying, and we would rather you did the arithmetic than take our word for it.

The hard questions

The objections worth answering properly.

“Won’t the agency just ignore the ruling?”

Some will, the first time. What changes is that the disagreement stops being your word against theirs and becomes a document with reasoning in it that they have to argue against in writing — and that they know is on a record neither of you can edit. In practice the useful moment is the next contract, where the clause goes in before outreach starts.

“Is this binding?”

Only if you both elect for it to be. The default is advisory: an expert determination on the evidence supplied, not arbitration and not a legal ruling. Binding is available, and is required where a fee sits in escrow.

“What if we are the ones who are wrong?”

Then the ruling says so, and you found out for the price of one adjudication, before it became a relationship problem. One of the three published specimens is exactly this case — the buyer who commissioned it lost it, and the fee stood.

“Who is actually deciding?”

You are told who the adjudicator is, and what else they do, before you send a single piece of evidence — and you may refuse them at no cost and without giving a reason. That disclosure goes to the parties, not to the internet, which is how it works everywhere it carries weight.

“Can this be used against us later?”

Individual rulings are private to the two parties. An agency’s rating is published only with that agency’s written consent. Aggregate anonymised data may be published at any time, and never identifies a buyer.

“What about the prospect’s data?”

Minimised. Prospect email addresses are hashed on receipt and never stored in plaintext. Name and job title are retained only where they are needed to evidence criterion 1. Evidence is purged twenty-four months after the ruling.

What does an adjudication actually buy?

Seven separate decisions on one meeting, the evidence relied on for each, the reasoning for any criterion that failed, and a statement of what would have changed the answer. Issued as a single document to both parties at the same moment, with fourteen days for either side to reply on the record.

It does not buy an opinion on whether the meeting was any good, a view on either party’s conduct, or a recommendation about your agency.

How fast, really?

Two business days from the point both sides have filed — not from the point you send it. Expedited same-day carries a supplement and is subject to capacity; it is refused rather than rushed when capacity is not there.

A genuinely borderline criterion may be held over to a second sitting a week later. That is slower on purpose: a hard call decided in fifteen minutes is a coin flip with a letterhead.

Do we need our agency to agree to this?

For a binding ruling, yes — both parties sign the adjudication agreement. For an advisory one, the agency still needs to file its side, because a ruling made on one party’s evidence is refused under commitment 02.

In practice the easier route is the contract clause: agree the definition and the referral route before outreach starts, when neither side knows which way any given meeting will go.

What happens to a meeting that is ruled not qualified?

The fee for that meeting is returned to you under the terms you and the agency agreed. The agency’s submitted count rises by one and its cleared count does not — which is how a rating moves.

Millesimal does not collect, hold or transfer that money in Phase 1. It determines the question; you and the agency settle it.

Is this arbitration?

No. It is an expert determination on the evidence supplied. It does not determine any contractual dispute between you and the agency, and it is not a legal ruling. Liability is capped at the fee paid for the adjudication concerned. Governing law is England and Wales.
