Hard questions

What buyers ask before they start.

“Will the agency just ignore the ruling?”

Some will, the first time. But then they must answer a reasoned ruling in writing, on a record neither side can edit. The bigger gain is the clause in your next contract.

“Is this binding?”

Only if you both choose it. By default it is advisory. A binding ruling is required when a fee is held in escrow.

“What if we are the ones who are wrong?”

Then the ruling says so, before it harms the relationship. In one of our three published specimens, the buyer lost and the fee stood.

“Who decides?”

Before you send evidence, we tell you who the adjudicator is and what else they do. You can refuse them at no cost and without a reason. This is shared with the parties, not published.

“Can this be used against us later?”

Rulings stay private to the two parties. A rating is published only with the agency’s written consent. Anonymised totals may be published, and never identify a buyer.

“What about the prospect’s data?”

We keep as little as possible. Prospect emails are hashed on receipt, never stored in plain text. Name and job title are kept only where criterion 1 needs them. Evidence is deleted after 24 months.

What do we get from an adjudication?

Seven decisions on one meeting, each with its evidence. Each failed criterion comes with the reasoning and what would have changed it. Both sides receive the document at once, with fourteen days to reply on the record.

You get no opinion on the meeting’s quality, either side’s conduct or your agency.

How long does it take?

Two business days from when both sides have filed. A ruling on the same day costs extra, and only if we have capacity.

A borderline criterion may go to a second sitting a week later. Hard calls are not rushed.

Do we need our agency to agree to this?

Yes. Both sides sign the adjudication agreement for a binding ruling. An advisory one still needs the agency’s evidence, as commitment 02 rules out deciding on one side’s alone.

Easier still, use the contract clause. Agree the definition and referral route before outreach starts.

What if a meeting is ruled not qualified?

You get that meeting’s fee back, on the terms agreed with the agency. The agency’s submitted count rises by one and its cleared count does not. That is how a rating moves.

In Phase 1, Millesimal does not handle that money. You and the agency settle it.

Is this arbitration?

No. It is an expert determination on the evidence supplied, not a legal ruling on your contract. Liability is capped at the adjudication fee. The law of England and Wales applies.
